ABOUT THIS WEBSITE

About Collection Explorer

A museum-style student project for exploring selected public catalogue records.

About these datasets

The first source is a National Museum of Australia Collection API search for Aboriginal, covering objects, archives and related records. The second is a screened selection from the State Library of Western Australia’s Digital Photographic Collection, a public dataset of historical photographs dated mainly from 1818–1950. A keyword match does not establish Indigenous authorship, cultural authority or connection to a particular Country.

Titles, captions and catalogue fields retain the institutions’ original English. No cultural stories, language vocabulary or historical interpretations have been generated for the catalogue.

Reading historical catalogues

Historical records may contain deceased people’s names or images, and outdated or offensive terms. The SLWA subset was selected with Aboriginal, Noongar, Nyungar and related keywords, then screened to exclude selected ceremonial, restricted, funerary, human-remains and nudity terms. Automated screening cannot replace community or cultural review. Consult the source institution and your facilitator before wider reuse.

English and Noongar

This prototype pairs an English interface with selected, published Noongar words. These are learning labels, not a complete Noongar translation. Noongar is associated with south-west Western Australia; the two source samples cover wider places and communities. A language label does not identify the language or Country of a record.

Dates and places

NMA production years use fields labelled Date made, Date created or Date of production. SLWA years use Date/s of creation. Unparsed dates retain their original wording. Place labels are not inferred to be Country, and missing data remains visible in the analysis.

Collection Explorer uses open catalogue data from the National Museum of Australia and the State Library of Western Australia.

This independent student prototype is not affiliated with either institution. Its visual direction draws on the collection displays of the National Museum of China and Tokyo National Museum.
